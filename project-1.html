GROUP PROJECT 01

Soccer meets DIY.

A playful connection between different interests, built around two buttons and the consequences they share.

PROJECT

A Family of Buttons

COLLABORATOR

Daniel Tangtakoune

MY ROLE

Concept development & implementation

TOOLS

Sketching · HTML/CSS/JS · Generative AI

01The interaction

Working with Daniel Tangtakoune, I developed an interactive prototype connecting my interest in soccer with his interest in DIY and home repair. “Kick Ball” damages a shared wall, while “Repair Wall” restores it. Both actions affect the same object, connecting our different hobbies through a playful interaction.

The submitted prototype is preserved below. The more gradual deterioration discussed later is a proposed improvement.

02Developing
the concept

Our expected three-person team became a two-person team, which required us to reconsider the concept around two buttons. I proposed moving gaming from the hobby represented by one button into the overall interaction style. This allowed us to retain a shared interest in gaming while giving each button a distinct hobby to express.

My initial idea paired kicking a soccer ball with repairing it. However, reflecting on my experience playing soccer indoors and in backyards, I realized that surrounding objects and walls were more susceptible to damage than the ball itself. I proposed shifting the damage to a wall, giving the repair action a clearer connection to DIY.

Daniel produced the sketches, and we jointly agreed on the visual choices and interaction rules. I implemented the application with generative AI assistance for framework guidance and other implementation support.

03Moving from
sketches to interaction

The wall’s deterioration and collapse were harder to implement than I anticipated. The animation I imagined was more complex than what I achieved in the prototype. Translating the idea into a working application exposed the gap between envisioning an interaction and communicating it convincingly through movement.

After seeing the working version, I felt the page contained too much text. I reduced it and refined visual details, including the soccer ball’s appearance, to give the interaction more space.

04 / REFLECTION & NEXT STEPS

Make each kick
feel consequential.

The critique highlighted opportunities to develop the interaction further. I agreed that the wall’s deterioration could be more gradual: a few distinct damage states conveyed its condition, but the changes felt larger than the slow breakdown I had intended.

In a future iteration, I would introduce smaller changes between states, such as cracks spreading and individual bricks loosening before collapse. I would then check whether those changes make each kick feel consequential while keeping the wall’s condition easy to understand.

This project helped me recognize that animation is part of how an interface communicates cause and effect. I want to evaluate that communication earlier in future projects, alongside the concept and visual design.
